Oh boy, here comes the hard part. For the electronics, you'll need:

- **4 piezo sensors**
- **Leonardo Arduino / Arduino Micro** (or knockoff)
- **Cable** to connect Arduino to computer (typically micro-USB to USB-A)

<Callout type="tip">
  If you're tech savvy, you can also get a numpad and figure that out yourself for extra input buttons.
</Callout>

## Wiring Overview

Now depending on how you're doing this, this can be pretty easy or pretty hard.

Essentially, all you need to do is connect the piezo sensors to **A0-A3** (one wire from each piezo to one pin on the Arduino) and the other wire from each piezo sensor to **3.3v**. So you could just tape the wires together, or if they have proper connections, just plug them in.

## Extending the Wires

The issue I had is that the piezo sensors were a bit short, so I bought a set of dupont wires, cut one end off, and soldered them to the piezos to extend their length.

Additionally, I cut 4 females, and soldered them all together to a male, so that way I could just plug in each piezo sensor to this wacky 1-to-4 wire connected to the ground.

## Alternative: Breadboard

Instead of wiring things directly to the Arduino, you could get the dupont wires and connect everything with a **breadboard**. This could be easier, as you would mostly just plug things into the breadboard — no soldering needed — but you'll need to learn how a breadboard works.
